# Behaviour Questions
1. Describe a time when you handled multiple IT tickets at the same time. How did you prioritize them?
- During my time with Ultimate IT Support, there was a situation where several support tickets came in around the same time. They included a user locked out of their account, a printer issue, a software installation request, and multiple users reporting slow network performance.
My responsibility was to prioritize the tickets based on their business impact and urgency while ensuring users were kept informed.
I first reviewed each ticket and used the priority and SLA guidelines to determine which required immediate attention. The network issue affecting multiple users was treated as the highest priority because it had the greatest business impact. I acknowledged all tickets, updated users that their requests had been received, and then worked through them in priority order. While investigating the network issue, I continued updating the remaining tickets and escalated issues whenever necessary.
As a result, the critical network issue was addressed quickly, the remaining tickets were resolved within their SLA, and users appreciated receiving regular communication throughout the process. The experience reinforced the importance of prioritization, clear communication, and staying organized when managing multiple requests.

2. If the CEO's email is not working and at the same time the company printer is down, how would you prioritise and handle these issues?
- I would first assess the business impact of both issues. In this situation, I would prioritise the CEO's email because email is a critical communication tool, especially for senior leadership who may be communicating with clients, partners, or other executives.
I would quickly acknowledge both users so they know their issues are being addressed. For the CEO, I'd start with some basic troubleshooting, such as checking Outlook connectivity, the internet connection, mailbox status, or whether the issue is affecting only one device.
For example, if I found that Outlook wasn't syncing because of a profile issue, I'd repair or recreate the Outlook profile. If the issue required Exchange or Microsoft 365 administration, I'd gather the necessary information and escalate it to the appropriate team while keeping the CEO updated.
Once the CEO's email was working or had been escalated, I'd move on to the printer issue by checking whether it was affecting one user or the whole office, verifying the printer's network connection, print queue, and status before resolving or escalating it if needed.
This approach ensures that the highest business-impact issue is handled first while maintaining good communication with everyone involved.
